Where Tick falls short. To be fair:

  • Tick is young and made by one person. The big apps have more people and more years behind them.
  • No voice assistant: no Siri, no Google Assistant.
  • No priorities, labels or filters. Todoist and TickTick do much more there.
  • No app of its own for Windows or Mac, as Todoist and Microsoft To Do have. There is the web app, which you install as an app in Chrome or Edge.
  • No link with Outlook tasks or Teams, as Microsoft To Do has. Tick can read your Outlook calendar, though.
  • On an iPhone: no reminders at a place, no widget and no quick add.

In the right place

A reminder when you get there

Some things you can only do once you're somewhere. Buying milk at the supermarket. Taking the parcel when you leave the house. Give a task a place on the map and choose On arrival or On leaving. Your phone watches that place itself, and Tick lets you know the moment your phone says you're there. And when you're behind the wheel, the screen gets bigger and simpler.

  • Pick a place by searching, dragging the map, My location or a favorite. Radius from 200 m to 2 km.
  • Swipe the notification away and it comes back the next time you arrive. Tick off marks the task done.
  • Already notified and you want it again? Tap Notify again.
  • A shared task with a place: everyone gets their own notification. Whoever passes the shop first does the shopping.
  • Driving mode: when Tick notices you're driving, you see big buttons, tasks Nearby and what's due today.
  • Your position is not sent to Tick. Your phone only reports that a reminder went off.

Place reminders only work in the Android app. On an iPhone and in the browser you can still set the place; Tick works there with everything except place reminders.

While driving, you shouldn't squint at your screen, so NICE AND BIG.

Shared or copy

Do it together, or each your own

You can share a task with someone in two ways. Shared is one task for both of you: done is done for everyone. Handy for the groceries, or the bins that need to go out. Copy gives the other person their own copy. You each tick off your own, and you see how far the other is. Handy when you both have to do the same thing, like your tax return. You can also share a whole list. Then you tap Take it and the others see you're on it.

Tickmanager

Old tasks get a second chance

Every list has them: tasks that sank to the bottom months ago. The Tickmanager brings them back one by one, oldest first. For each task you choose: Schedule, Move to top, Leave it or Delete. One tap, and on to the next. An orange button shows how many are waiting. So you tidy your list in a few minutes, without having to take in everything at once.

A new task is old after 14 days. Pinned tasks, repeating tasks and tasks with a reminder in the future don't take part. Works offline too.

All features

First the features you notice every day. Tap a tile for the whole story.

⏰Reminders

Today, tomorrow or next week, in one tap.Read moreClose

Reminders

Tap Remind me… on a task. At the top is Pick day and time. Below it are quick choices for today, tomorrow and next week. A new reminder starts at the next whole hour. Pick only a date, and Tick uses your default time: 09:00, unless you changed it.

At that moment you get a notification on every device where you turned notifications on. It has two buttons: Done ticks the task off, In 1 hour moves it an hour later. Tap the notification itself to open the task.

The reminder is also the deadline. There is no separate due date. In the list, the time is in grey under the task. Once it has passed, it turns red.

Every day at 09:00 Tick sends a notification for overdue tasks, with Done and Tomorrow. At most ten, oldest first. You can change that time, or turn it off.

Notifications can be up to a minute late, because Tick checks once a minute.

🔁Repeating

Every week on Wednesday, until a date if you like.Read moreClose

Repeating

At a reminder, choose under Repeat how often: every day, week, month or year. Or every so many minutes, hours or days, on certain weekdays, on a day of the month, or on, say, the third Sunday. An end date works too.

Tick off a repeating task and it moves on to the next time. It stays open, and its subtasks reopen.

A missed time is skipped. The next time is always in the future, so you don't get a pile of catch-up notifications.

Snooze, or choose Tomorrow, and only this one time moves. To change every time, change the rhythm under Repeat.

10:00 stays 10:00 when daylight saving time starts. “On the 31st” falls on the last day of shorter months.

☀️Due today

What's due today, from all your lists, at the top.Read moreClose

Due today

At the top of your list is a bar Due today with a number. That's how many tasks are due today, overdue ones included. When something is overdue, the bar is red.

Tap the bar and it folds open. You see every task due today from all your lists, shared ones too, earliest first, with the name of its list. Open a task, or tick it off right there. Each device remembers whether you left the bar open or closed.

The same number is on the app icon and on the browser tab. The small tab icon gets a red dot when something is overdue. So you see at a glance whether something is waiting, without opening the app.

Nothing due today? Then the bar is gone. Rather not see a number? Turn the counter off per device in Settings.

🗓️Date from your text

Type ‘tomorrow 3pm’ and Tick sets up the reminder.Read moreClose

Date from your text

Just type the day and time in the title, the way you would say it: call the dentist tomorrow 3pm. Tick spots it while you type and fills in Remind me…, marked from your text. When you add the task, those words leave the title. The task is then called call the dentist, with a reminder tomorrow at 15:00.

It works in English and Dutch, whatever language the app is in: tomorrow, tonight, friday, next week, in 2 hours, 24 nov, at 1500, 8pm or December 31st. A day without a time gets your default time. A number on its own stays text: order 1500 flyers gets no time.

Not what you meant? Tap Ignore and your words stay as they are. If you pick a time yourself, that always wins. Quick add and the widget in the Android app don't read dates from your text yet.

📍Places

AndroidA notification when you arrive or leave.Read moreClose

Places

Give a task a place and Tick lets you know when you arrive or leave. The shopping list at the supermarket. The screws when you park at the hardware store. The parcel as you step out the door.

Tap Remind me at a place on a task. Search for an address, drag the map, tap My location or pick a favorite. Choose On arrival or On leaving, and the radius: from 200 m to 2 km. A place you use often, you keep with ☆ Save as favorite.

Not the right moment? Swipe the notification away and it comes back the next time you arrive. Notify again re-arms a place that already went off.

Your phone watches the places itself and only tells Tick that a notification went off. Tick keeps no record of where you've been.

Place reminders only work in the Android app. On an iPhone or in the browser you can still set the place. Maps come from OpenStreetMap.

More explanation and screens under In the right place.

👥Shared lists

One list together, and you see who does what.Read moreClose

Shared lists

The groceries for the house, the packing list for a weekend away. Share a whole list and every task in it is shared with everyone in the list. Choose Share… in the list menu and invite someone. They accept once and have the whole list.

Everyone can add, change, tick off and delete. Tap Take it under a task and the others see “… is on it”. Only you can tick it off then. Tap again to let go.

A ticked-off task doesn't vanish. It moves to the Ticked off block at the bottom, with who did it and when. Clear moves them to the trash for everyone.

By default you get no notification when someone else ticks something off. Turn on Notify when ticked off for the whole list, or choose Notify me when this is ticked off per task.

Drag a task to one of your own lists and it's yours alone. Drag one of your own tasks in and it belongs to everyone. Only the person who shared the list can invite people, remove them or delete the list.

Shared or copy, explained with two phones: see Shared or copy.

☑️Subtasks

2/4 done, right in your list.Read moreClose

Subtasks

Break a big task into small steps. A task can have one level of subtasks. In the list they share one grey block: the subtasks smaller and lighter, with a thin line to the task above. Next to the title you see how many are done, such as 2/4.

A subtask follows its task. It's in the same list, and when you tick off the task, the subtasks go with it. Tick off a subtask and it drops to the bottom within its task.

In a repeating task the subtasks reopen as soon as the task moves on. Handy for a fixed round, like the weekly cleaning.

Share a task as a copy, and the other person's progress shows in the bar you see. Their subtasks themselves you don't see.

📱Widget and quick add

AndroidYour list on your home screen.Read moreClose

Widget and quick add

Put the Tick widget on your Android home screen. You choose what it shows: all tasks, Today and overdue, or one list. You set the text size and line spacing, and make it as big as you like.

Tick tasks off right on the widget. You get three seconds to undo. Tap a task to open it, or the header to open the app on that list. Each row shows what belongs with it: reminder, list, place, repeat, sharing and open subtasks.

With ➕ on the widget, the New task shortcut on the app icon, or Share from another app, a panel opens at the bottom. Type the title and pick a list, a reminder, a place or a repeat if you like. Turn on Add another one after this to type several tasks in a row.

The widget and quick add are only in the Android app.

📴Works offline

Changes go along once you're back online.Read moreClose

Works offline

In the basement, on the train, abroad without data. Tick works without a connection, once you've opened it online and logged in.

Adding, changing and ticking off tasks just carries on. Tick saves it on your device and sends it once you're connected again. If two devices changed the same thing, the newest change stays.

In a shared list you can add, tick off and Take it offline. If two people take the same task offline, whoever comes online first gets it.

What needs a connection: sharing, the trash, attachments and the map. The Tickmanager works offline, with the tasks already on your device.

📅Calendar

A month view and an agenda of what's coming.Read moreClose

Calendar

Calendar in the sidebar shows your open tasks with a date. In two ways: as a month grid, with bars on the days that have something, and as an agenda, day by day. What's overdue is at the top.

Repeating tasks show with their coming times. So you also see the bins three weeks from now.

The week starts on Monday or Sunday, as you choose in Settings. With a linked calendar, your appointments show as grey blocks between your tasks. You can't open those in Tick; they're there to show when you're busy.

Tap a task to open it and change the time.

✍️Entry card

Type a task and add what belongs with it straight away.Read moreClose

Entry card

Adding a task starts with typing in the field at the top of your list. Press Enter and it's in. Want more? The field opens into a card. It has a short sentence for each part: Remind me…, Repeat, Remind me at a place, the list it goes into, and Do it together with…. A sentence in grey is still empty. Tap it and choose.

Under the sentences is Description, subtasks or attachment. There you write notes, add subtasks, or attach a photo or file. You can also paste a picture or drop a file on the card.

Add a day or time as you type, such as tomorrow 3pm, and Tick sets up that reminder for you. See Date from your text. You can also choose yourself, with Pick day and time or a quick choice for today, tomorrow or next week.

With a keyboard: N opens the field, Shift+Enter adds the task and opens it right away, Esc empties the card. Open the task later and you see the same sentences. What you filled in shows in colour.

📝Notes

With headings, links and lists to tick off.Read moreClose

Notes with checklists

Every task has a Description. Put in whatever you like: an address, a link, a shopping list. You can use headings, bold, italics, lists, links and quotes (if you know it: it's markdown).

Start a line with - [ ], as in - [ ] milk, and it becomes a box you can tick while reading the note. So you have a quick list inside a task, without every item needing its own subtask.

Tables and pictures in the text show as plain text. Title and notes save while you type.

Search looks in your notes too, including those of completed tasks.

📎Photos and files

Add them to any task.Read moreClose

Photos and files

A photo of the paint colour, the receipt, the manual as a PDF. Tap Photo or file on a task, or paste or drop it in. Tap a photo for a large view, with previous and next.

Photos are scaled down before they're uploaded. On the way, Tick removes the hidden data, such as where the photo was taken.

At most 20 files per task, each up to 10 MB. At app.ticktodo.nl you have 100 MB per account. Settings › Data › Storage shows how much you use.

Deleting an attachment can't be undone. Attachments need a connection; photos you've already seen stay visible offline. A backup lists which attachments there were, but doesn't contain the files.

🤝Sharing tasks

Shared to do together, or a copy for each.Read moreClose

Sharing tasks

Open a task and tap Do it together with…. Pick someone and choose how.

Shared is one task for everyone. Done is done for everyone, everyone can change it and add subtasks, and each person keeps it in their own list. A reminder at a time or place reaches everyone. You recognise it by the blue people icon and Shared with ….

Copy gives the other person their own copy, with all subtasks open. You each tick off your own. You recognise it by the grey icon of two sheets and a name with a progress bar. When the other is done, you get a notification “… is done ✓”.

The other person gets a notification with Accept and Decline. An accepted task lands in their Inbox.

At app.ticktodo.nl you share with an email address. If that person has no account yet, they get an invitation by email. You confirm your own email address first. You share with up to 3 people: as many tasks and lists as you like.

⚡Tickmanager

Old tasks come back one by one.Read moreClose

Tickmanager

Tasks you haven't looked at in a long time sink to the bottom of a list. The Tickmanager brings them back. An orange lightning button (on a wide screen: Tickmanager under Inbox) shows how many are waiting.

Tap Start tidying up and you see one task at a time, oldest first, with how long you haven't looked at it. For each task you choose: Schedule, Move to top, Leave it or Delete. At the top you see how far you are, and Stop lets you continue later.

A new task is old after 14 days, a task you already looked at after 30. You can change both. Pinned tasks, repeating tasks and tasks with a reminder in the future don't take part. You choose per list whether it takes part.

In a shared list, “looked at” counts for everyone. Which lists you skip is yours.

Works offline too. Rather not? Turn off Show Tickmanager in Settings.

🔗Link your calendar

Your appointments next to your tasks. Tick only looks.Read moreClose

Linking your calendar

Link your online calendar and Tick knows when you're busy. When you pick a time for a reminder, you see your appointments in grey between the free half hours, as in the picture, and in the Calendar they sit between your tasks. The quick choices for a reminder never land on an appointment: Tick moves them to the first free moment that day. With Pick in your calendar you see your days side by side. When an appointment later lands on a task, you get a notification with Reschedule and Next free time.

What Tick sees, and what it doesn't. Of each appointment only the title, start and end, from today up to six weeks ahead. No description, no location, no attendees. Appointments marked free and cancelled ones are skipped.

Tick only looks. It can't add, change or delete anything in your calendar. Your appointments go to no one else and are not used for ads. Every fetch, about once an hour, replaces the previous copy: old appointments are not kept.

You keep the key. The secret address is stored encrypted and never shown again. Unlink erases everything Tick fetched at once, and your appointments are not in the backup. Don't want Tick to see even the titles? In Outlook, choose Can view when I'm busy when you publish: Tick then only gets busy times.

To link: in Tick, open Settings › Calendars, paste the secret address of your calendar from Google Calendar, Outlook, iCloud or another calendar (the so-called iCal address), and tap Link calendar. Up to ten calendars, for example Work and Home.

The other way round: Add to calendar makes a 30-minute appointment at the reminder time, in Google Calendar or as a file any calendar app can open. That works without linking.

🗓️Pick in your calendar

See your days side by side and tap a half hour.Read moreClose

Pick in your calendar

With a calendar linked, the reminder menu has a second line: Pick in your calendar. You see your days side by side, top to bottom in half hours. Three days on a phone, swipe for more; a whole week on a computer.

Your appointments are in grey, with their title. Tick's suggestions are yellow half hours with a star; the other free half hours are blue buttons. A task that already has a reminder shows as grey text in its half hour.

Tap a half hour and that's the time. During an appointment is fine too: Tick never blocks it, the reminder then says during with the appointment's name. You can always pick again. You look two weeks ahead, up to six weeks with More days. Also in the Tickmanager under Schedule.

Works once you have linked a calendar (see Linking your calendar).

🛒More than one place

More than one place

You don't have just one supermarket. Give a task up to 10 places and Tick lets you know at the first one you arrive at (or leave). Once, not again at every shop.

Pick a place on the map and tap + Another place. Move the map to the next one. The places you already chose are under Also at; tap one to move it, and ✕ removes it.

You choose the places yourself. Tick doesn't look up shops for you and doesn't know where you are: your phone watches the places itself.

The notification comes in the Android app from version 1.13.0. A phone watches at most about 100 places at once. If you have more, the app shows which tasks aren't being watched.

🚗Driving mode

Big and simple behind the wheel.Read moreClose

Driving mode

When Tick notices you're driving, the screen gets bigger and simpler. You see a bar Driving mode detected, with tasks Nearby (within 3 km, closest first) and what's due Today. Every task has a big round button to tick it off, and there's a big Add task button.

Tick switches it on when the app is open and you've been going faster than about 25 km/h for half a minute. Three minutes of standing still or walking, and the normal view comes back. A traffic light or a jam doesn't count.

Sitting next to the driver, or on a train? Tap Not now. Never turns it off on all your devices.

For this, Tick uses your location only on this device, only while Tick is open. It's not sent to the server and not stored. Tick never asks for your location out of the blue.

Please don't look at your screen too long while driving. That's exactly why it's big.

🔗Share to Tick

Share to Tick

A recipe, an article, concert tickets: share the page from your browser to Tick. The page name becomes the task, with the link in it. If there is a date in it, Tick suggests a reminder. You check it and tap Add.

Tick is in your phone's share menu with the Android app, or when you install Tick as an app from Chrome on Android. On an iPhone it can't be done: copy the link and paste it into a new task.

📦Moving from TickTick or Todoist

Bring your tasks along.Read moreClose

Import from TickTick or Todoist

Coming from TickTick or Todoist? Make an export there: in TickTick a backup (.csv), in Todoist a project (.csv) or the full backup (.zip). In Tick, choose Settings › Data › Get tasks from another app.

You see what comes in first: how many tasks, which lists, and what can't come along. Only when you tap Import does Tick add them.

Lists, subtasks, notes, dates and most repeats come along. Priority becomes a line in the note; a high priority also becomes a pinned task. Changed your mind? Undo last import moves the whole import to the trash in one go.

💾Backup

Download all your tasks in one file.Read moreClose

Backup

Under Settings › Data you download a backup of all your tasks. Completed tasks and places are included. Your settings, keys, tasks others shared with you and the files of attachments are not.

In the same place you put such a file back. Tick first shows what's in it. Then it merges by task: tasks in the file replace the same task here, and deleted ones come back. Nothing is deleted.

A backup is also your way out. Your tasks are yours, and you take them with you if you want.

Your account

🚩Reporting and blocking

Got something that shouldn't be there? Gone.Read moreClose

Reporting and blocking

At app.ticktodo.nl anyone can make an account. So it can happen that someone sends you something you don't want. That's what Report is for.

You can report an invitation, a task someone shared with you, a shared list (Report list), or a file someone else added. Choose a reason: spam, inappropriate, illegal or something else, and add a note if you like. It disappears from your Tick straight away. The administrator gets an email and looks at it.

Also block is ticked by default. Blocking stops everything you share with that person, both ways, and their invitations no longer reach you. They aren't told. You can also choose Only block.

In copies of the same task you no longer see each other's name and progress, and you don't get each other's “… is done ✓”.

The people you blocked are under Settings › Account › Blocked people. You can lift the block there. Then you see each other again in copies you both still have. What was shared doesn't come back by itself, but you can share again.

On a Tick you host yourself for your household, these buttons don't exist. There you know each other.

🏠Home Assistant

A notification when something happens at home: someone comes home, the sun sets.Read moreClose

Home Assistant

Use Home Assistant at home? Then a task can notify you at a moment from your house instead of at a time: when Sam comes home, when the sun sets, when the bathroom light turns on. So you hear about the school trip form when your daughter walks in, not at three o'clock in a meeting.

You make the moments (triggers) once in Home Assistant, with the Tick integration: a name, what Home Assistant watches and the state, optionally with only after 5 minutes or only between 22:00 and 06:00. In Tick you tap Notify on a Home Assistant trigger on a task and pick one or more.

The first one that goes off sends one notification, to you and to everyone the task is shared with. After that it says Already notified · Notify again, so a light that goes on twenty times a day doesn't nag. A repeating task waits for the trigger again each time.

Only one person in the house needs Home Assistant. Under Settings › Home Assistant you share your triggers with people you already share a list or task with; they then pick them on their own tasks. Tick only gets the names and when a trigger went off, nothing else about your home. For now you install the integration in Home Assistant by hand; how is in the guide on GitHub.

🔌Integrations

Claude, Home Assistant and an inbox for your own automations.Read moreClose

Integrations

For people who like to tinker, Tick has a few ways in.

Claude (MCP): Tick has an MCP server, so Claude Code or Claude Desktop can manage your tasks: search, add, change, tick off, snooze and move them to the trash. Claude uses a key of its own that you can revoke at any time. It can't reach your settings, keys or password.

Inbox API: with an Inbox key you add tasks from a shortcut, a script or an email rule. With title, notes, date, list and repeat. Such a key can only add tasks, nothing else.

Home Assistant: a task that notifies you when something happens at home (see Home Assistant). And sensors for your open, today's and overdue tasks, with actions to complete, postpone and add tasks, in one package file.

Fallback webhook: when a notification couldn't be delivered within 15 minutes, Tick can send it to a webhook of your own, for example a chat app.

You make keys under Settings › Advanced › Keys. You see a key once, and revoking works immediately.

🚲On time on the road

GitHub appOn time by bike or car.Read moreClose

On time on the road

Android only notices you've reached a place a few minutes later. On foot that hardly matters. By car or bike you're past it by then.

In the Android app, under Tick on this phone, turn on On time on the road. While you drive or cycle, Tick makes arrival circles bigger. The notification then comes around the edge of the circle you set, and says “Almost at …”.

How much bigger, Tick learns from your earlier notifications, separately for car and bike. A circle never grows so big that you're already inside it when you set off. When you get out or off, the circles go back to normal.

Tick asks for the permission Physical activity, only to know whether you drive or cycle. It's not a fitness feature. Nothing about where you are or how you travel is sent.

This is only in the Android app from GitHub, not in the Google Play version. Why is under Independent.

Frequently asked questions

Question not here? Email dio@ticktodo.nl.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Tick is free and has no ads. On app.ticktodo.nl you share with up to 3 people. More under Does it cost anything?

Does Tick work on my iPhone?

Yes, as a web app. Open app.ticktodo.nl in Safari, tap the Share button and choose Add to Home Screen. Tick then opens like an app, with notifications (iOS 16.4 or newer). On an iPhone, place reminders, a widget and quick add don't work. Sharing a page from Safari to Tick isn't possible either: copy the link and paste it into a new task. Tick doesn't work with Apple Reminders, because Apple doesn't let other apps in there. You can simply share a list with someone on Android, though: you both see the same thing, and done is done. There's no App Store app: that needs a paid Apple account and a review for every update.

Can I try Tick without an account?

Yes. Try it now, no account needed gives you your own Tick straight away, with sample tasks and a shared list, no email address or password needed. The demo disappears an hour after you last used it, and at the latest three hours after it started. Like it? Choose Create account and your tasks stay.

Do I lose my tasks when I get a new phone?

No. Your tasks are on the server and on your devices. Log in on your new phone and everything is back. The same goes if your phone breaks.

Who is behind it?

Me, Dio, in the Netherlands. Tick at app.ticktodo.nl is offered by Dio Communicatieadvies. How Tick is paid for and what happens if I stop is under Who is behind it.

What's the difference between shared and copy?

Shared is one task for both of you: done is done for everyone, and everyone can change it. Delete it and it disappears for everyone (30 days in the trash); only want to be rid of it yourself? Choose Stop sharing. A copy is the other person's own copy, with the title, the description and all subtasks open. After that it's their task: you only see each other's progress, a name with a small bar or a ✓. You can still share a copy as long as the other person hasn't accepted it: pick the same person and tap Change. The other way round, turning a shared task into a copy, does work.

Does the other person need an account?

At app.ticktodo.nl you share with an email address. If that address has no account yet, it gets an invitation by email, with a link to sign up. The invitation is then waiting for them. An invitation stays open for 30 days. You confirm your own email address before you can share.

How many people can I share with?

On app.ticktodo.nl with up to 3 people, with as many tasks and lists as you like. What others share with you doesn't count. So you can always accept an invitation.

What if there are more than 3 of us?

A shared list then has you plus 3 others: 4 in all. Everyone can also share with 3 others themselves, because what others share with you doesn't count. More people in one list isn't possible on app.ticktodo.nl. On a Tick you host yourself for your household, that limit doesn't apply.

Is there an Android app?

Yes, free on Google Play: see the Android app. There's also a version outside Google Play; it's under Independent.

How reliable are notifications on an iPhone?

They work when Tick is on your home screen (iOS 16.4 or newer) and you allow notifications; in Safari itself you don't get them. Tick sends them the same way as to any browser; Apple does the delivering. A place reminder doesn't reach an iPhone. Want a safety net? Under Settings › Advanced you set a fallback webhook: if a notification isn't delivered within 15 minutes, Tick sends it there, for example to a chat app.

I'm coming from an iPhone. Is Tick the same as Reminders?

Mostly. Lists, subtasks, reminders at a time and sharing are all there. A reminder at a place only comes in the Android app. Tick has no Siri and isn't built into your phone the way Reminders is built into an iPhone. On the other hand, Tick also works in your browser, on any device. And the Tickmanager helps you clear out old tasks instead of letting them sit there for months. How Tick compares to other apps is under Tick next to other apps.

I'm coming from Google Keep. Do my lists come along?

Not by themselves. Tick can only bring in tasks from TickTick and Todoist. A Keep list you type over, or you paste it into the description of one task; put - [ ] in front of a line and it becomes a box to tick off. Turning every pasted line into its own task isn't possible yet.

Can I move from TickTick or Todoist?

Yes. Make an export in your old app and choose Settings › Data › Get tasks from another app in Tick. You see what comes in first. Lists, subtasks, notes, dates and most repeats come along; a high priority becomes a pinned task. Not happy? Undo last import removes the whole import in one go. More under Moving from TickTick or Todoist.

Can I take my tasks to another app?

You can always download all your tasks under Settings › Data, as a backup in one file. That's plain text (JSON) you can open and read, but other to-do apps won't just read it in. Moving to another app takes some retyping or pasting. See Backup.

Are there priorities, labels or filters?

No. Tick sticks to lists, subtasks, tasks you pin to the top, and search (in notes and completed tasks too). If you work a lot with priorities and labels, Todoist or TickTick suits you better.

Is there an app for Windows or Mac?

Not a separate one. On your computer you use Tick in the browser, at app.ticktodo.nl. In Chrome or Edge you can install it as an app, with its own window and icon.

Is Tick suitable for work or a team?

For your own work list and a small group, yes: you share with up to 3 people. For a team it lacks things: no priorities or labels, and no link with Outlook tasks or Teams. A team can host Tick itself, though, with no limit on the number of people.

Does Tick know where I am?

No. Your phone watches your places itself and only tells Tick that a notification went off. Driving mode uses your location only on your device, while Tick is open. Tick only keeps the places you save yourself.

Can the administrator read my tasks? And where are they stored?

Technically, yes. Your tasks are not end-to-end encrypted, so whoever runs a Tick can get to them: on app.ticktodo.nl that's me, on your own Tick its administrator. The admin screen only shows counts, no content; only with an abuse report does the administrator see what was reported. On app.ticktodo.nl the database with your tasks is at Cloudflare, in Western Europe; Cloudflare is an American company, though. More in the privacy policy.

Does Tick pick up a date from what I type?

Yes. Type tomorrow 3pm, friday or in 2 hours in the title and Tick suggests that reminder. Those words then leave the title. Ignore keeps your text as it is. This works in the app on the web, on Android and on the iPhone. Quick add and the widget in the Android app don't do it yet. More under Date from your text.

Can Tick remind me at every supermarket?

No, on purpose. You choose up to 10 places per task yourself, for example the three supermarkets you go to most. That way Tick looks nothing up in the background and your location goes nowhere. See More than one place.

How do I delete my account?

In the app: Settings › Account › Delete account. You confirm with your password. Then everything goes right away, except an anonymised record of abuse reports; backups are erased within 30 days. Can't log in anymore? See app.ticktodo.nl/delete-account. Download a backup first if you want to keep your tasks.

Can I host Tick myself?

Yes. The code is on GitHub (DioVayne/tick), under the MIT licence. You put it on a free Cloudflare account; with Claude Code that takes about fifteen minutes. Then you can invite everyone in your household. Your own Tick is a separate server, though: you can only share with people on the same Tick. More under Independent.
